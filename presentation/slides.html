Proof

It works on the real sky

Barnard’s Star — the fastest-moving star in the sky — visibly crosses our frames over 10 real epochs: built-in ground truth.

The 5σ detector flags it; Gaia DR3 labels known stars automatically — human attention goes to the uncatalogued.

16 automated tests, incl. a synthetic injected mover recovered at >5σ while a steady star is correctly not flagged.

The full demo runs offline from cached archive data — no live network needed on stage.

Transparency

AI use, scientific validity, honest limits

AI use — declared

Claude (Anthropic) — AI assistant co-wrote and refactored application code, under human review and testing.

OpenCV — classical computer vision powers an optional visual-aid scan, kept strictly separate from the scientific pipeline.

No AI-generated images, video or audio anywhere. Every image is a real SPHEREx observation or a derived product of one.

Validity & limits

WCS alignment → variance-normalized difference → 5σ threshold → Gaia cross-match. Flags are unclassified candidates, never “discoveries”.

Stated limits: display-grade resampling, detector wavelength shifts between visits, ~6-month cadence.

Independent verification one tap away: NASA WiseView and ESASky at each candidate’s coordinates.
